============================================================
RECURSIVE CHARACTER TEXT SPLITTER
============================================================

WHAT IS RECURSIVE CHUNKING?

Recursive chunking tries to preserve natural text structure while
keeping chunks within the desired chunk size.

Instead of blindly cutting text every N characters/tokens, it tries
larger meaningful boundaries first.

Conceptually:

Paragraph
   ↓ if too large
Line
   ↓ if too large
Word
   ↓ if too large
Character


------------------------------------------------------------
WHY IS IT CALLED "RECURSIVE"?
------------------------------------------------------------

Because the splitter keeps trying smaller separators when the
current piece of text is still too large.

Example:

Try "\n\n" → Paragraph boundary
      ↓ still too large

Try "\n"   → Line boundary
      ↓ still too large

Try " "    → Word boundary
      ↓ still too large

Try ""     → Character boundary


Interview Definition:

RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter recursively tries smaller separators
until the text can fit within the desired chunk size.


------------------------------------------------------------
DEFAULT SEPARATORS
------------------------------------------------------------

Conceptually:

["\n\n", "\n", " ", ""]

Meaning:

"\n\n" → Paragraph boundary
"\n"   → Line boundary
" "    → Word boundary
""     → Character boundary

The splitter prefers the earlier separators because they preserve
more natural text structure.


------------------------------------------------------------
IMPORTANT BEHAVIOR
------------------------------------------------------------

Finding a separator DOES NOT mean every separated piece automatically
becomes one chunk.

Example:

chunk_size = 100

Paragraph A = 40 characters
Paragraph B = 30 characters
Paragraph C = 50 characters

The splitter can combine smaller pieces:

Chunk 1:
Paragraph A + Paragraph B
≈ 70 characters

Adding Paragraph C would exceed the chunk size.

Therefore:

Chunk 2:
Paragraph C


------------------------------------------------------------
THREE IMPORTANT SETTINGS
------------------------------------------------------------

1. chunk_size

Controls the target maximum size of each chunk.

Example:

chunk_size = 500


2. chunk_overlap

Controls how much previous content is retained between neighboring
chunks to help preserve context.

Example:

chunk_size = 500
chunk_overlap = 100


3. separators

Controls WHERE the splitter prefers to break the text.

Example:

["\n\n", "\n", " ", ""]


Easy Mental Model:

chunk_size
→ How large should my chunks be?

chunk_overlap
→ How much previous context should I retain?

separators
→ Where should I preferably split the text?


------------------------------------------------------------
WHY NOT ALWAYS USE SMALL CHUNKS?
------------------------------------------------------------

Too-small chunks can break related information apart.

Example:

Chunk 1:
Python was created by Guido van Rossum.

Chunk 2:
Python is widely used in AI.

A question requiring both facts may need multiple chunks.


------------------------------------------------------------
WHY NOT ALWAYS USE LARGE CHUNKS?
------------------------------------------------------------

Large chunks can contain many unrelated topics.

This makes the embedding less focused and can reduce retrieval quality.


------------------------------------------------------------
CHOOSING CHUNK SIZE
------------------------------------------------------------

There is NO universal perfect chunk size.

Bad:

"Always use 500 tokens."

The correct chunk size depends on:

- Document structure
- Amount of context needed
- Type of information
- Types of user questions
- Embedding model
- Retrieval strategy


Example:

Legal contracts
→ May require larger chunks because clauses can be long and depend
  on surrounding context.

FAQ documents
→ Can often use smaller chunks because each question/answer may
  already be self-contained.


------------------------------------------------------------
KEY IDEA
------------------------------------------------------------

RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter tries to:

KEEP RELATED INFORMATION TOGETHER

while also:

KEEPING CHUNKS WITHIN THE DESIRED SIZE.


------------------------------------------------------------
INTERVIEW QUICK REVISION
------------------------------------------------------------

Q: Why use RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter?

A:
It preserves natural text boundaries better than blindly splitting
at fixed positions.


Q: Why is it called recursive?

A:
It progressively tries smaller separators when a piece of text is
still too large.


Q: What does chunk_size control?

A:
The target maximum size of the resulting chunks.


Q: What does chunk_overlap control?

A:
How much previous context is retained between neighboring chunks.


Q: What do separators control?

A:
Where the splitter prefers to break the text.


Q: Is there one perfect chunk size for every RAG application?

A:
No. Chunk size depends on the documents, queries, context requirements,
embedding model, and retrieval strategy.
